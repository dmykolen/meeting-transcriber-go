THREE EXPERIMENTS ON "a planning meeting" (recording 74, 23 min)
========================================================================

The repetition the owner reported is NOT echo. It is in the transcript, and
Whisper put it there. As shipped, this meeting contains "Данію." ten times in a
row, one row per second, from 15:31 to 15:42.


A — WHISPER SETTINGS                                    A-whisper-74.txt
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  setting                          rows   loops   longest run   speed
  as it ships                       281      10          10x     44x
  max_text_ctx = 0                  210       0           —      56x   ← fix
  temperature fallback only         281      10          10x     33x
  max_text_ctx 0 + fallback         210       0           —      35x
  the same + beam 5                 210       0           —      21x

  One setting is enough. max_text_ctx = 0 is whisper.cpp's name for
  "condition_on_previous_text = False": the decoder stops being fed its own
  previous output, so it cannot lock into a groove. It removes every repetition
  and is FASTER than what ships. The temperature fallback does nothing at all
  here, and beam search only costs time.


B — PARAKEET INSTEAD OF WHISPER                        B-parakeet-74.txt
------------------------------------------------------------------------
                    rows   loops   cyrillic letters   russian letters   speed
  parakeet            11       0               5701                 0    8.1x
  whisper            281      10              10367                 0   11.4x

  Parakeet does not loop — it structurally cannot. But it transcribed eleven
  rows for a twenty-three minute meeting, roughly one every two minutes, and
  what it produced is largely nonsense: "Тому це дефої теперізм, виконувати
  агресію". Half the letters of the Whisper run and none of the meaning. It is
  also slower.

  The earlier worry — that Parakeet decides a Ukrainian meeting is Russian —
  did not repeat here: zero Russian-only letters. It is simply much worse on
  this material, and slower.

  Verdict: not a replacement.


C — THE TWO CHANNELS TRANSCRIBED SEPARATELY                C-split-74.txt
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  microphone (the owner)      406 rows,  1m41s,  no clustering ran at all
  system tap (everyone else)  260 rows,  2m17s,  5 speakers
  both, interleaved           666 rows,  1 repetition (a real one: "Ні, ні, ні.")

  Scored against what the owner heard:

    3:04–4:56  he says Сергій            → tap says SPEAKER_02
    4:56–4:59  he says Kovalenko Ostap  → tap says SPEAKER_02
    5:00–6:12  he says Сергій            → tap says SPEAKER_02

  The two Сергій stretches agree with each other and come back as a speaker of
  their own rather than as somebody the app already knows. That is the correct
  answer for a person nobody has enrolled, and it is exactly what the shipping
  pipeline gets wrong: today both stretches are labelled "Kovalenko Ostap", a
  stranger folded into an enrolled person, which nothing downstream can undo.

  The three-second Kovalenko interjection at 4:56 is missed. It is shorter than
  the diarizer's min_duration_on and sits inside a long stretch of one voice.

  What C does NOT fix on its own: the microphone hears the far side through the
  speakers, so the microphone transcript also contains their words. Adding the
  two transcripts together double-counts. That is a routing decision per row —
  which channel owns this moment — not sample-level echo cancellation, and it
  is a much smaller problem than the one that has been chased for days.


D — A SWITCH INSTEAD OF A SUM                              D-switch-74.txt
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The owner worked out in one sentence what days of DSP did not: the recording
  is fine, the mixing is not. media/fold.go ends with

      out[i] = system[i] + mic[i]*gain

  which is a SUM. The microphone is ducked to 0.25 while the far side talks but
  it is still added, and it still holds their voice coming back off the
  speakers. Every remote sentence is therefore in the mix twice.

  mix                                ECHO median   audible   YOU
  fold as it ships (duck 0.25)           -19.1 dB      99%   -1.8 dB
  fold, ducked to 0.05                   -20.5 dB      98%   -1.8 dB
  fold, ducked to 0.02                   -21.4 dB      96%   -1.8 dB
  switch, not aligned                     -8.7 dB      89%   -0.0 dB
  switch, tap 200 ms earlier             -16.7 dB      74%   -0.0 dB
  switch, tap 230 ms earlier             -23.3 dB      55%   -0.0 dB   ← best
  switch, tap 260 ms earlier             -15.1 dB      79%   -0.0 dB

  Two things to read here.

  Ducking harder does almost nothing (-19.1 to -21.4 dB for a twelve-fold
  change) and costs the owner's own voice 1.8 dB every time, because it is
  still a sum. A switch costs his voice NOTHING — 0.0 dB in every case — since
  at any instant only one channel is in the mix at all.

  And the switch only works aligned. Unaligned it is worse than the fold: it
  changes hands a quarter of a second late at both ends of every sentence. The
  sweep peaks at 230 ms, which is the same figure experiment 4 arrived at by a
  completely different route.

  What is left at -23.3 dB is double-talk: while both are speaking the
  microphone legitimately owns the moment and carries their room copy with it.
  That is the part a real echo canceller would finish — and now it would
  converge, because the channels line up.
