# Recurring unnamed voices

Generated by `go run ./exp/20_voices` on 2026-09-29 from a private archive snapshot. People are P1…P7 by how many meetings they were named in.

## Method

1. A voice is the stored voiceprint of one label in one meeting; `SPEAKER_NN` is unnamed, a label equal to an enrolled person is named, the owner's `Me`/`You` is left out.
2. Only prints of the current speaker model (192-d) are compared; older meetings hold prints of a previous model, and re-embedding them from audio was checked below and rejected.
3. Calibration takes cosines between named voices, of which 50 of 55 were named by the recogniser itself (app log) and so are not independent truth; a name-free check re-embeds each label's alternate turns as two halves.
4. Average-linkage agglomerative clustering over all voices (merge while the mean pairwise cosine ≥ t); B-cubed on named voices; merged same-meeting label pairs as a label-free impurity signal.
5. An unnamed cluster holds more unnamed than named speech; clusters rank by unnamed hours; share and coverage are of all unnamed speech in the archive.

## What this says

Within one meeting the current model tells voices apart: a label's two halves meet at a median 0.83, two labels of one meeting at 0.36. Across meetings the named voices do not hold together: same-name pairs sit at a median 0.45 against 0.40 for different names, P1's 38 voices scatter over 11 clusters at t = 0.55, and the centres of the people named in five or more meetings are 0.78–0.84 alike. 50 of the 55 names came from the recogniser itself at 0.55, a bar that 14% of different voices within one meeting clear, so they are not truth, and B³ precision of 0.75–0.77 is a best case. Of the five largest unnamed groups at t = 0.55, 2 hold named voices of two or more people and 3 hold no named voice at all, which leaves their purity to the ear. P2 and P5, named only in older meetings, have no enrolled print the current model can compare, so every later appearance of theirs is unnamed by construction. The older meetings cannot join either: re-embedding them collapses distinct labels together.

Naming a recurring voice once is not safe as "apply to every member". It can work as "confirm by ear on the least typical members, then apply above a strict bar": at t = 0.70 the clustering merges 3.7% of one-meeting label pairs against 10.8% at 0.55, and 77% of one voice's halves still clear it.
